# Corrections: editing, gaps

How a gap divides an entry on its line: making it, filling it with another profile, and leaving it. The popup and the
break each have their own file beside this one. Each decision follows the template in `../../README.md`, and is cited by
its title.

## A gap divides an entry (2026-11-24)

**Built by:** Corrections.

**Decision:** with both handles inward ("Editing an entry on its line"), a click on the middle, where the pointer shows
it can be clicked, makes it a gap instead of a shortening, removing the middle and splitting the entry in two, its time
nobody's, as between a stop and a later start; a second click shortens again. The handles are then the gap's edges. A
gap holds until it is left on purpose, in one of three ways: a click on it, which shortens again; its edges let go
together, which makes the entry whole; or an edge let go at the entry's start or end, which drops the part on that side
and leaves a shortening of what remains. A drag alone never leaves it: the gap's edges stay inside the entry, each
stopping at the entry's start or end, or sooner where a part's break stops it, and reaching an end only while the other
part keeps at least a minute, so what is left is an entry ("An edited entry's break"). The edges keep no distance
between them: the dragged one snaps to the other, even off the 5-minute marks, as a handle snaps to a neighbour's edge
in docking ("Two entries join by docking"), and the parts show as one; dragged back out, the gap returns. While a let go
would leave the gap, its edges meeting or an edge at the entry's end, the part not dragged is outlined, 1 px just
outside it, in the entry's base colour, as a docked neighbour is outlined in the colour of the stretch reaching it. Let
go there, or stepped there by the minute, the gap is left: the entry whole, or what remains of it, its handles at its
start and end as the view refits. Once left, moving both handles inward shortens again, so an old gap never comes back
unasked. A gap shows a red middle. With a gap, the profile picker fills it: another profile picked makes the middle a
new entry of that profile between the two parts, and the entry's own profile picked empties it again. Each control
undoes only its own work: a filled middle is a new entry, not a gap, so it shows no pointer and a click on it does
nothing; the picker empties it first. A filled middle wears that profile's colour, and the two parts turn grey, as any
neighbour on the line. Its edges are the new entry's, kept 5 minutes apart as any entry's handles, and go past the
entry's edges: dragged to an edge of the entry, the new entry takes that end and the part on that side goes; its handles
go on, to the next entry or now, the time beyond the old edge green as added. Dragged over both edges, the whole entry
is the other profile's, unless the entry's break holds a part back. A switch between a gap and a shortening that the
break cannot fit in is refused, and so is a profile picked that it cannot fit in; the tag shows why, red until the next
change. Under the day, a divided entry shows each part's times and tag, set apart by a centred dot, in white while the
parts are the entry's own; with the gap filled by another profile the text follows the line: the parts grey, and the new
entry's time, with no name, in that profile's base colour ("08:00–10:00 paus 20min · 10:00–10:40 · 10:40–12:00"); the
centred dot appears only between entries.

**Why:** a shortening is the common fix, so it is the default, and the gap is one click away for an errand found
afterwards. A gap left only on purpose keeps the handles' meaning fixed for the whole drag: they are the gap's edges
until the owner lets go somewhere that says otherwise, and a break's wall cannot be skipped by dragging past it. A gap's
edges meeting close it as docking joins two entries, and the same outline says what a let go will do; its colour is the
stretch reaching the other piece, green when docking adds time, the entry's own here. The click on the middle stays the
switch between gap and shortening, each control undoing only its own work.

**Rejected:** a gap by default: a break found afterwards has the tag, so a gap is rarer than a shortening. A row of
choices under the line (shorten, gap, another profile), with no default and the confirm waiting on one: a row of
controls and a click for an edit the line already previews. A hint in words on the middle ("klicka: hål"): cryptic to a
newcomer; the pointer is enough. The head's picker greyed while a gap holds another profile: with the colour rule it
names what is in colour, so it never means two things at once. Writing a forgotten entry over an existing one, cutting
its way in: the line's wall at other entries would have one exception, and a gap filled by another profile does the same
from inside the entry. A gap closed only by a click on it: that click switches to a shortening, so nothing gave the
whole entry back, and the dragged edges stopped 5 minutes apart. A gap ending as soon as an edge left the entry:
mid-drag the handles turned from the gap's edges into the entry's ends, the gap flipped into the kept stretch, and a
part's break was skipped. An edge let go at the entry's end keeping the gap's stretch and dropping the part beyond, so
the handles stay put, the red stretch outlined to say so: the last step of a drag that removed time would keep it and
drop what was kept, and the outline would contradict the red it surrounds. The handles gliding to their new places when
a gap is left, or only the one held, across to the far end: one more case for a rare moment, which the outline already
shows before the let go and the view's refit carries.
